## The Beginning: HTML, CSS, and a Lot of YouTube

I didn't start with a grand master plan. My coding journey officially began in my first year of college with a simple HTML, CSS, and JavaScript project. I remember watching a YouTube tutorial about building a basic webpage and thinking, *"I could do that."*

Spoiler: I could not do that. Not yet.

Like many beginners, I relied entirely on those videos just to get a button to work. I made the classic rookie mistake: I fell straight into **tutorial hell**. I was watching videos and typing exactly what the instructor typed, but I wasn't really absorbing *why* the code worked. I was just happy it didn't crash.

## The Laravel Awakening

Fast forward to my 3rd year of college, specifically in my Application Development class. The major requirement was to build an e-commerce application, and the required tech stack was PHP and Laravel. I didn't choose the frameworks, so I had to figure them out fast. 

Guess what? I got stuck in tutorial hell again. I basically built exactly what the tutorial video built, step by step, just to meet the requirement. But something different happened this time. When I looked at the finished, working application, it sparked a realization. Even though I had hand-held my way through the code, I saw the potential of what I was doing. I made a quiet promise to myself right then: *"Someday, I am going to be able to build something like this entirely on my own, without someone telling me what to type."*

## The Internship Leap (Armed with Basics and Courage)

The real turning point came when I applied for an internship at an IT company affiliated with my school. At the time, my toolkit consisted of HTML, CSS, basic JavaScript, and honestly... just a lot of courage lol. To my surprise, I was accepted.

My very first task was a simple HTML, CSS, and JS assignment to test my baseline knowledge. I put my head down, gave it my best effort, and managed to pass. 

Because of that, I was assigned to be part of the Frontend Developer team. The project? Developing a tablet-based Point of Sale (POS) mobile application from scratch. There was just one massive problem: I had absolutely zero knowledge of the modern frameworks required to build it. 

This is where the environment of a real tech team changed everything for me. The senior developers there didn't just hand me the answers or send me links to YouTube videos. Instead, they taught me the most valuable skill a developer can have: **how to read documentation.** When I hit a wall, I learned to read the actual official docs to understand the architecture, rather than searching for a quick fix. It was incredibly humbling, but it worked.

## Where I Am Now

Today, I am incredibly grateful to be working as a Full-Stack Software Engineer and AI Automation Engineer. 

I definitely don't know everything, and I am constantly learning every single day. But looking back, that internship broke my reliance on tutorial videos and gave me the confidence to tackle unfamiliar technologies. It taught me that it is completely okay not to know everything on day one, as long as you are willing to sit down and find the answers.

## Advice for Those Just Starting

* **Acknowledge Tutorial Hell:** It's okay to use tutorials to start, but stop just copying the code. Pause the video and try to write the next function yourself.
* **Apply Before You Feel "Ready":** If I had waited until I felt like a master programmer to apply for my internship, I never would have gotten the job. Bring the basics, a willingness to learn, and a lot of courage.
* **RTFM (Read The Friendly Manual):** Senior developers aren't magic; they just know how to read the documentation. Make it a habit to check the official docs before searching for a video fix.
* **Build It Anyway:** It's going to be ugly at first, but shipping real projects is the only way to truly learn.

The path isn't linear. Focus on yesterday's version of yourself, not someone with five more years of experience. Keep building!
